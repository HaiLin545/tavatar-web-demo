Abstract

Reconstructing high-fidelity and animatable human avatars from monocular videos remains a key challenge for immersive virtual applications. Although 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) enables efficient human modeling, existing methods often optimize Gaussians with unconstrained geometric attributes (rotation or scale). As these Gaussians typically float freely in space and lack a coherent topology, they easily overfit to training poses under the monocular video setting. This makes it difficult to accurately model surface stretching and compression during novel pose animation. To overcome this, we propose Tavatar, a topology-aware Gaussian binding framework that establishes explicit geometry constraints between 3DGS and parametric human mesh. To ensure robustness under deformation, we align Gaussians with a coarse clothed mesh that can be animated. Specifically, Gaussians are bound to both mesh faces and vertices, oriented along mesh normals. Face Gaussians use analytically derived scales from triangle geometry, while Vertex Gaussians have learnable scales constrained by local edge lengths, ensuring full surface coverage with topology-aware constraints. To further preserve mesh quality, we introduce an equilateral regularization term that minimizes triangle aspect ratio distortion during optimization.

Overview

Overview of the Tavatar framework. We begin with a canonical SMPL mesh and use a shape encoder to predict a coarse deformable mesh. This mesh guides our dual Gaussian binding strategy: Face Gaussians provide structured surface coverage, while Vertex Gaussians enhance local detail. All Gaussians inherit topology-aware scale and rotation attributes from the mesh to maintain consistency during animation. The overall optimization combines photometric and geometric losses, including a novel equilateral constraint that promotes stable scaling and complete surface coverage.
